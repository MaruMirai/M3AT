# Skinning and drawing the figure

Step 3 of the asset pipeline. With `Skinned` on (`WalkFigures.cs`) and the files present, `Source/SkinnedFigure.cs` skins the exported mesh (`skin.txt`, `anim.txt`, `garment_*.txt`; `dev_notes/clips.md`) per pawn. Otherwise `WalkFigures.cs` falls back to the old baked frames: reads `clip.txt`, loads each frame through `Api.LoadModel` and draws one per frame with `Api.DrawModel`. Which garment is drawn for which apparel (own garments, stand-ins): `dev_notes/apparel.md`. How the weapon is held and drawn: `dev_notes/weapons.md`.

## Posing

- A mesh is skinned in a frame only when its bones or shape changed (`Finish`: `ReadBones` copies each bone's top three rows to `Posed.boneRows`, which `Pose` skins from, as the engine's `MultiplyPoint3x4` transposes the matrix every call). `posedAt` counts the changes; `Wearing` adds one for what places a backpack. **Anything else a mesh's vertices depend on must bump `posedAt` too**, as setting vertices rebuilds the whole mesh and its GPU buffers.
- Shapes: one per body type, head shrink and the other keys (`ShapeOf`, cached; `dev_notes/body.md`). `End` corrects the clips down the bone chain for that shape's moved joints.
- Clips: idle blended into walk by IsoRim's gait, between frames and the gait levels either side of the pace. Turns are blended parent-relative (`ReadAnim` converts them) and `End` rebuilds the chain, so `Over` can lay a clip's arms, chest and head over any gait (`dev_notes/clips.md`'s `over` lines). The idle and drafted loops run on game ticks, offset per pawn (`LoopPhase`). Other layers: `Top` (over what `Over` blended), `Nudge` (additive from a clip's first frame), `Override` (a ragdoll's turns).
- `fewerPoses` (off): a figure is posed only every second or third draw by how many are on screen (`PoseEvery`, `figuresLast`, `WaitsItsTurn`, at most `PoseLongest` apart; never a carried one, `poseEveryDraw`), `bones`, `whole` and `pos` put back to the last pose meanwhile (`shownBones` ...) so what rides them stays with the hands; place and yaw still set every draw.

## GPU skinning (`Source/GpuSkinning.cs`)

`gpuSkinning` (on; `SkinnedFigure.GpuSkinning`; also needs our own materials in use and the skinned light pass shader, `GpuWorks`) poses the body, garments, fur, hair, beard and horns on the graphics card:

- `Pose` calls `GpuPose`, which sets each mesh's rest pose in the pawn's shape (the outline's pushed out along its hull) and, once, each vertex's bones (`PackedOf`: up to `MostBones` (12) a vertex, each one number bone + weight / 2, in UV channels 2 and 3 and the tangent, the only extra channels the engine carries), only when the shape changes, with bounds of `GpuBounds`, so a new pose sets nothing.
- `DrawMeshes` → `DrawParts`: each part by `Api.SubmitFigureMesh` (stacked floor and fade, IsoRim's outline option, floor and hit flash written into the block, `MeshPart.lit` for IsoRim's light pass, `NoCull`) with the pawn's block (`BlockOf`: `_MarusBones`, three rows per bone, set only when `posedAt` changes, plus `_IsoOwnerFloor`). `MarusSmooth.vert.glsl`/`MarusSmoothTex.vert.glsl` skin by it (`Skin`, `Influence`, binding 6, declared in `MarusSmooth.frag.glsl` too, as the engine reads the layout from the fragment stage). A mesh with no packed bones is drawn as it is.
- Each figure is culled whole by `FigureOnScreen` (`Api.FigureVisible` on `GpuBounds`), as IsoRim's per-mesh test would use the engine's stale box.
- IsoRim's light pass for `MarusSmooth` materials gets `MarusSmoothMark`'s program (`ConfigureFigureMaterial`'s `lightPassShader`, `SkinMarkShader`), else it would light the mesh at rest.
- What reads posed vertices (`Extent`, `SeatOf`, the wounds) gets them from `PosedOnCpu`, without setting a mesh. The face patch, wounds, cat ears and tail and a backpack are still posed on the CPU.
- Off: `LeaveGpu` empties a mesh's packed bones and `Pose` sets vertices again.

## Garments drawn and hidden

- A textured garment is drawn with `MarusSmoothTex` (`TexturedMaterial`, shared with the hair) times IsoRim's apparel colour only if made from stuff or dyed (`Tinted`, or its `tint` line), else as it is (power armour). Its outline is its image's mean colour.
- The body is drawn without the triangles worn garments hide (`HideUnder`, one list per mix of garments, cached).
- Each worn garment is drawn without its triangles under one that goes over it (`HideGarments`, `GoesOver`: an outer layer, or on the same layer the one on the torso over the one not, so a shirt over trousers). Worked out in the game, once per pair at rest in the base shape (`UnderOf`: a line straight out from the inner garment, from `GarmentInside` under to `GarmentLook` out, meets the outer one, or the corner is on one of its vertices, `OnVertex`, so a duster's open front shows the shirt; not over the outer's `opens` (`OverOpens`), so a shirt's cuff fills the parka's; where either rides one bone whole (a hat, a mask) only corners with `RidesWith` of their weight on that bone or below count, `RiddenBone`/`WeightBelow`; the fur's tufts the same, `FurUnder`; the exporter's test is `HIDE_RIDES_WITH`), cached per garment and mix.
- A garment hiding more than half the scalp leaves the hair off, more than half the face patch the face layers (`FindHeadHiders`, `HidesHair`/`HidesFace`; its `hide_hair`/`hide_face` lines decide instead).
- Visors: a garment with `visor` lines draws its solid without them (`shellTris`, `GarmentSolidTris`; the outline keeps them) and the glass as a piece of its own (`VisorOf` at `Load`, posed as hair, `Posed.visors`, shape in `Shape.hairs`), not a sub-mesh (Waylith's `Mesh` rebuilds from sub-mesh 0's triangles alone whenever vertices or colours are set). `DrawVisor` draws it with `MarusGlass` (`GlassMaterial`, `GlassColour`; `GpuSkinning.DrawSubmesh`): `MarusSmooth.frag.glsl` with `_Glass` 1 (more opaque and lighter at the edges, `GlassRim`, a glint, `GlassShine`), alpha-blended at queue 3000 with no depth write, after the face layers and wounds, so the light passes behind it (3099) light it too.

## Hats and hair

- Tucking (`hide_hair tuck`: the `make_hats.py` hats, the plate helmet): the hat hides the scalp triangles above its rim (`WithScalpUnder`), and `Source/HairUnderHat.cs` keeps the hair: `WornHair` (one copy per style and hat, the first such hat worn, made at rest in the base shape) measures the hat each way round the head (`RimOf`: its lowest within `BrimReach` of the skin, and its lowest at each distance out, `under`), cuts the hair and shell `RimTuck` above that (`Clipped`: triangles cut through, new vertices mixing texture place, normal and keys) and draws the rest in toward the head's vertical line (`DrawnIn`: no further out than the rim plus `HairFlare` per cell below, nor than the hat at that height, `UnderHat`, a smooth minimum over `FlareEase`, never past the skin). The shell's `hide` keeps only what it still encloses (`StillInside`). The scalp stays hair-coloured.
  - A way round with no rim (lowest point near the skin within `RimCrown` of the skin's reach, up by the crown) only fills gaps with a rim on both sides (`FillRim`); under a hat that doesn't go all round, only scalp triangles a line from the head's middle meets the hat over are hidden (`OverHead`).
- Over the hair (`over_hair` line, `GARMENT_PROPS` `over_hair` with `hide_hair` off: the slicecap, hairbands, ribbons, maid headdress): the hair is left whole and the garment pushed out past the style worn (`Source/HatOverHair.cs`: `HairUnderHats`, from `DrawDressed` after `Wearing`, gives the figure the hair (`Posed.hairOver`, a change bumping `posedAt`); `OverHairShape`, in `Pose` after `GarmentShape`, cached in `Shape.overHair`: each place along its line from the head's middle to `OverHairClear` over the outermost hair on it, `OutermostAlong`, held over `OverHairHold`, tapering at `OverHairSlope`, averaged over `OverHairBlend`, never below a place's need), as tucked under its crescent the scalp showed round it.
- Head shrink on garments (`Source/EvenShrink.cs`, `EvenHeadShrink`, at `Load` after the mod garments): each garment vertex takes `Shrinkhead` as one even scale fitted to the head's own move, by its weight on the head bone (the exporter's move for the rest), as the exporter's fit kept a hat's full-size gap while the hair shrank. Hair drawn in under a rim (`Clipped`) takes its old place's `Shrinkhead` move less `headScale - 1` times how far it was drawn in.

## Materials and shaders

- `MarusSmooth` (`Defs/ShaderDefs/MarusSmooth_Shaders.xml`, `Shaders/MarusSmooth.*.glsl`, `SkinnedFigure.SmoothShading`, always on) is a copy of the *installed* IsoRim's `IsoRim.vert.glsl`/`IsoRimSolid.frag.glsl` (**when IsoRim changes those, copy the changes across**; `./check_isorim.sh` lists them first). Its changes: sun shade from the skinned vertex normals rather than IsoRim's snapped per-triangle normal, and the outline's ground lift. IsoRim's M229 is in (`ToEye`, also in `MarusFace.frag.glsl`). `ShadeBands` ≥ 2 rounds to toon levels. The face patches get the same normals and `MarusFace` shades them the same way (`_Smooth`).
- Every material of ours is set up by `Api.ConfigureFigureMaterial` (also for IsoRim's peek fade on stacked floors), then given the figure shader contract's values (`_LightMode`, `_GlowMode`, `_MarkPass`, `_OutlinePush`; IsoRim Docs/Api.md part 43). Without our shaders, or until IsoRim's materials exist, the pawn is left to IsoRim (`SkinnedFigure.MaterialsReady`).
- Draws go through `Api.SubmitFigureMesh` with our own outline hull: the mean of the face normals round each vertex (`Hull`; `Api.BuildOutlineHull` is IsoRim's mitred one).

## Outlines (`Source/OutlineOrder.cs`)

- All the chibi's outlines use `MarusOutline` (IsoRim's `IsoRimOutline` state on the `MarusSmooth` program): `_OutlinePush` = `SkinnedFigure.OutlinePush` (0.05 cells; IsoRim's is 0.2); a corner under the ground is slid back along its view ray to 0.006 cells over it (under the water sheet), so terrain doesn't cut the outline at the feet. Own light pass `MarusOutlineMark` (IsoRim's outline has none and took the pulled overlay at night).
- Silhouette: drawn only where stencil bit 64 is clear, which `MarusSmooth`/`MarusSmoothTex` set, so the outline is the silhouette alone (64 is the one bit neither the game nor IsoRim uses). So one chibi's body doesn't hide another's outline, each figure in a frame (`SkinnedFigure.NextFigure`, from `DrawDressed`) gets its own run of four queues from 2002 (`Slotted`, per-slot material copies, `Slots` of them): solids, textured, outline, then `MarusStencilClear` clearing the bit over its solid and outline meshes. A held weapon shares the figure's outline (`dev_notes/weapons.md`, `DrawJoined`).
- `outlineMode` `Lines` (`SkinnedFigure.Silhouette` false) draws `MarusOutlineLines` instead (no stencil test, IsoRim's push). `ScreenSpace` and the other outline options: `dev_notes/options.md`.
- Item outlines on the ground (`Source/GroundOutlines.cs`): IsoRim's `ModelBaker.Write` clamps outline corners to the ground, hiding the ring of a thing lying flat; a prefix and postfix lift item outline corners at that height `OutlineLift` cells (items only). Not patched in once IsoRim has its own fix (M230, its `IsoRimOutlineRing` ShaderDef). The only code using IsoRim internals besides the screen-space outline's depth grab.

## Occlusion and shadows

The figure takes neither IsoRim's AO (`IsoRimAO`, `IsoRimAOBlur`, queues 2450–2451: normals from depth, so per flat triangle, and under the face layers, so the lid cover showed as a mask) nor its screen-space shadows (`IsoRimSSS`, `IsoRimSSSBlur`, 2452–2453; full-screen passes that paint the picture back, so nothing of ours can share their queues). Each solid mesh is drawn again at 2454 (`AfterOcclusion`, `MarusSmoothAfterAO`, `MarusSmoothTexAfterAO`, no depth or stencil write; `PieceMeshes.afterAt`, left out while neither pass is drawn, `OcclusionShows`); its depth is still in the image, so it casts shadows. `clothesOcclusion` (on, `SkinnedFigure.ClothesOcclusion`) redraws only the skin (`PieceMeshes.isSkin`: body and fur), so clothes and hair take both. IsoRim's models on the figure and its outline still take them.

## Hit flash (`Source/HitFlash.cs`)

`hitFlash` (on): the game's red damage flash. `HitFlash.Of` (the pawn's `DamageFlasher` in sixteenths, none for another mod's draw, `ctx.ForOtherMod`; per figure, not `ctx.Flash`, as a carried chibi is drawn inside its carrier's call) is set in `DrawDressed` (`Posed.flash`, `FaceLayers.Flash`) and passed to `Api.SubmitFigureMesh`, which writes `_IsoFlash` (green and blue × 1 − it): body parts, face layers, ridges and flat beard. `MarusSmooth.frag.glsl` leaves outlines (`_OutlinePush` ≠ 0) and `MarusFace.frag.glsl` wounds (modes 6, 7) unflashed. Weapons, tools and worn gear don't flash. Not yet checked in game.

## Faces

`Source/FaceLayers.cs` loads the face patches itself (IsoRim's loader can't take a per-pawn texture) and draws Facial Animation's layers on them: `dev_notes/faces.md`.

## Horns

`anim_scripts/make_horns.py` (`python3 anim_scripts/make_horns.py <chibi folder> [hair .txt ...]`; run by the export after `skin.txt`) makes `horns_mini.txt` and `horns_center.txt` from the head and `HORNS` (lengths in cells from the skin; root sunk `SINK`): longer than the sprites, as built hair stands up to 0.05 cells off the forehead, and steered away from IsoRim's 55° camera, which would see them end-on. Given built hair it prints each horn's `Clearance` past the hair. In the game: `dev_notes/body.md`.
